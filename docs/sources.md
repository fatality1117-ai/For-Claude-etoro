## Tier 1 — Named analysts

### 湯之上隆 (Takashi Yunogami) — Japan
**The primary Japan source.** Former Hitachi / Elpida / Selete process engineer, 16 years in
microfabrication, PhD Kyoto. Now head of 微細加工研究所 (Institute of Microfabrication
Research), consultant and journalist.

- Column: 「湯之上隆のナノフォーカス」 on EE Times Japan — `eetimes.itmedia.co.jp/ee/series/11164/` (at #93 as of Jul 2026)
- Also JBpress, Business Journal; own site `yunogami.net`
- Japanese only, which widens the gap — but language is not the point (see Audience below).

Why he matters here: his recent columns land directly on positions we hold.
- #93 (Jul 2026): the memory market "explosion" — argues the 10x expansion in three years is historically abnormal, and advises preparing for the downturn while conditions are peak
- Jul 2026: back-calculates a "collapse line" for AI datacentre investment from GPU, HBM and power costs
- Mar 2026: AI's competitive axis is shifting from semiconductors to power, and calls this Japan's strategic blind spot
- Apr–Jun 2026: helium and naphtha supply crisis as a risk to photoresist and therefore to global fab operation

He is frequently contrarian and has a track record of calling downturns early. Relaying him
gives the account a bearish counterweight it currently lacks — useful, because agreeing with
everything reads as having no view.

### 大山聡 (Satoshi Oyama) — Japan
Column 「大山聡の業界スコープ」, same EE Times Japan platform. Industry-structure and
corporate-strategy angle rather than process technology. Secondary to Yunogami.

### 郭明錤 (Ming-Chi Kuo) — Taiwan
TF International Securities. X is blocked, but `medium.com/@mingchikuo` is a live mirror of
his X and is fetchable.

He self-publishes in both Chinese and English, which does not reduce his value here.
Most eToro readers have never heard of him and would not find him unprompted. The product is
curation and explanation, not translation.

## Audience

**Write for someone who has never heard of these analysts and does not know what HBM is.**

The eToro audience is retail, and most of it is not specialist in semiconductors. The value
this account adds is not bridging a language gap — it is that we know who is worth reading and
can explain what they said. A reader who already followed Yunogami and Kuo would not need us.

This has three consequences for every relay post:

1. **Introduce the person in one line.** Credentials and track record, every time, even for
   repeat subjects. "Former Hitachi and Elpida process engineer, now an independent analyst
   who called the 2022 downturn early" does more work than the name alone.
2. **Explain the mechanism in plain language before the implication.** If a post uses HBM,
   wafer allocation, CoWoS, glass core substrate or similar, one short clause has to say what
   it is. Assume nothing.
3. **Do not dilute the substance to achieve this.** The edge is that we understand it. The
   deliverable is making it understandable. Simplifying the explanation is right; simplifying
   the analysis is not.

This applies to every post type, not only the relay column.

## Rules

1. **Named analysts only.** Anonymous "supply chain sources" and "market rumour" do not
   qualify — that is exactly the trade-press chatter the sourcing standard rejects. If the
   analyst is themselves assessing a rumour, the analyst's assessment is the citation, not
   the rumour.
2. **Paraphrase, do not translate wholesale.** Reproducing a source's article in translation
   is republication. Take the claim and the number, write the sentence fresh, attribute by
   name and date, and link.
3. **Check the date on every fetch.** Some personal sites serve stale index pages.
4. **Recency rules are unchanged.** Within 24h → MARKET PULSE framing. Within the week →
   THE ASIA READ. Older → background only, never the hook.
5. **Connect to a position.** A relay with no link to something held or watched is a news
   summary, and news summaries are not what this account is for.

## Gaps

- No Korean-language source. Deliberate — the account underweights Korea, and adding a
  Korea-heavy source would pull coverage toward positions not held.
- No Chinese-mainland source. Language access exists but the geopolitical framing makes
  relaying mainland analysts a reputational risk not worth taking.
- Sell-side Japanese equipment research (e.g. the Nomura/MUFG franchise) is not public.
  Only what reaches the press is usable.
